## Premise

In January 1959 ten students and graduates of the Ural Polytechnic
Institute, led by **Igor Dyatlov**, set out on skis for Mount Otorten in
the northern Urals. On 28 January **Yuri Yudin** turned back, ill. On the
evening of **1 February** the nine others pitched their tent on the
eastern slope of **Kholat Syakhl**, cutting a platform into the snow below
a small shoulder; their last photograph shows them digging it.

That night they left the tent. It was found on **26 February**, half under
snow, **cut open from inside**. Below it, footprints of eight or nine
people, in socks, in one felt boot, barefoot, went down the slope for
about 500 m at a walking pace, in a line, towards a large **cedar** at the
forest edge, where a small fire had burned. Six of the nine died of cold;
three, found in May in a ravine under four metres of snow, had severe
injuries to the chest and skull, as from a great weight. In 1959 the case
was closed on "a compelling natural force".

The gap filled with legends: an attack by Mansi hunters (dismissed: no
other tracks, no struggle), a yeti, aliens and orange spheres in the sky,
a secret weapons test, radiation. In **2020** a reinvestigation by the
Russian prosecutor's office concluded that **an avalanche** had most
likely driven them from the tent. In **2021** Johan Gaume and Alexander
Puzrin showed how a **small slab avalanche** could release on that gentle
slope, hours after the **cut** made for the tent, once katabatic wind had
piled snow above it, and how such a slab could cause those injuries to
people lying on a hard floor. Follow-up expeditions found the area
avalanche-prone. The authors themselves say no one knows for certain what
happened that night.

The keeper's jar holds the slab, the cut, and the walk to the cedar.

## Tone and guardrails

- **Nine real people, honoured.** Their names appear once, in the case
  file's list, as they were. No body is shown or described beyond the
  case file's own words (cold; severe injuries to chest and skull). The
  ravine is told in one line. No photograph of a person is shown; their
  last frames show their work, drawn without faces.
- **The Mansi theory is stated as dismissed,** in the investigators'
  words, without suggesting guilt.
- **The uncertainty is kept.** The 2020 and 2021 findings are the best
  account, not proof; the seal says what the record supports, and a later
  paper keeps the authors' own caution.
- **Legends are red herrings:** aliens and orange spheres, a yeti, a
  weapons test, radiation, the Mansi, "a compelling natural force".

## Where it is set

- **A. The slope during the search, late February 1959** (recommended):
  the tent half buried, the cut platform, the raised footprints going
  down, the cedar with its fire; the search camp's tent with the radio and
  the case file; the investigators' darkroom in Ivdel, where the films
  from the tent are developed. The 2020–2021 papers and the snow kit
  arrive as things "that should not be here yet".
- **B. The pass in winter 2021, a follow-up expedition:** the memorial,
  the slope as it is now; the 1959 film and case file arrive from the
  past. Truthful for the snow science, but the tent and the tracks are
  gone, and the tale is told only through papers.

## New mechanics (this tale only)

- **The pit (`snowpit`).** The height of the tale. Beside the tent, a pit
  dug down through the snowpack. Test each layer by pushing in a fist,
  four fingers, one finger, a pencil, a knife: the largest that goes in
  gives its hardness. Find the weak layer, soft under a hard slab. Then
  cut a column deeper than it and tap the shovel on top, ten from the
  wrist, ten from the elbow, ten from the shoulder: it breaks clean on the
  weak layer. A column cut too shallow tells nothing. Tactile and
  deductive; unlike the strand (`strand`) or the probe (`scan`), the layers
  are compared, not searched for a highest.
- **The darkroom (`darkroom`).** The films found in the tent. A test
  strip printed in bands of doubling exposure: choose the band where the
  snow is still white and the figures already dark, then print each frame
  at that time; frames printed too short come out grey and empty, too long
  black. The last frame, printed right, shows them cutting into the slope
  before sunset, the cut the slab broke above. Unlike Pompeii's lens (`lensHours`)
  or wiping (`reveal`): the picture is made by choosing, not uncovered.

## The seal

A new form, **`routebook`**: the group's route book, the booklet a
sports-club party carried and had stamped at each stage; its last page
was never filled in. The player writes the last entry.

"A small {slab} of snow broke above the {cut} made for the tent, and they
walked down to the {cedar}."

Decoys: aliens, a yeti, a weapons test, the Mansi, radiation, a compelling
natural force, Mount Otorten.

**Ending:** "The pass where they died bears Igor Dyatlov's name. In 2020
the case was closed again: an avalanche, most likely. What happened in
the tent that night, no one saw."

## Echoes and living things

- **Echoes:** a searcher with a long probe on the slope; an investigator
  under the red lamp. None of the nine is shown, as echo or otherwise.
- **Living things (northern Urals, February):** none on the open slope
  but the wind; at the forest edge, a raven in the cedar, larch and spruce
  under snow.

## As built

- **Map:** the slope (start) leads down to the cedar and across to the
  search camp; the darkroom in Ivdel opens from the camp once the
  cameras are taken.
- **Flow:** the tent, slit from inside; the tracks (*the cedar*). In the
  camp: the case file (*Mount Otorten*, *a compelling natural force*),
  the rumours (*the Mansi*, *aliens*, *a weapons test*), the cameras. In
  the darkroom, the films printed (`darkroom`, gives *the cut*). Then the
  snow kit beside the tent (`snowpit`, gives *slab*). Then papers from the
  future in the camp (*radiation*, *a yeti*; 2020 and 2021, and the
  authors' caution). The keeper's slip is tucked in the cedar's broken
  branch once those are read; the last entry is written in the route
  book in the camp.
- **The pit:** six layers: new snow (F, 12 cm), the wind slab (1F, 38),
  the weak layer (F, 6), old snow (P, 34), a crust (K, 4), depth hoar
  (4F, 22). The depth hoar under the crust is soft under hard too: a
  column cut below it breaks higher up, on the true weak layer, at the
  14th tap (the elbow).
- **The darkroom:** a strip of 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 seconds; four frames
  (the river, the cache, the open slope, the cut) at 8, 4, 16 and 8.
- **Words:** *slab*, *the cut*, *the cedar* (the answers); *aliens*, *a
  yeti*, *a weapons test*, *the Mansi*, *radiation*, *a compelling natural
  force*, *Mount Otorten* (decoys).
- **Echoes:** a searcher with a probe on the slope; an investigator under
  the red lamp. **Living things:** a raven in a tree at the forest edge
  (`raven`).
- **Audio:** music `ural_wind`; effects `wind_ridge`, `canvas_flap`,
  `radio_static`; interface `snow_push`, `shovel_tap`, `column_break`,
  `enlarger`.

## Things to watch when tested

- Whether the five pushing objects and "the largest that goes in" are
  understood without the hint, and whether the F/4F/1F/P/K shorthand
  helps or puzzles.
- Whether the decoy (soft under the crust) feels fair: the column breaks
  higher, and the message says to mark that layer.
- Whether the test strip's bands read at phone size, and whether "too
  short / too long" is clear from the picture alone.
- The tone of the case file and the later papers, for a reader who knows
  the case; the Russian names and the Russian text, for a native reader.
