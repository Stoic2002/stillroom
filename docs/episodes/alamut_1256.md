# Episode design: Alamut, 1256 (the library of the fortress)

Status: **built** (2026-09-30): draft text in seven languages, code-drawn
art (`lib/core/art/alamut_1256_art.dart`), generated audio, walkthrough
test. Approved by the developer with setting A, both mechanics, and seal
sentence (a). Shelf III (opens after 4 tales), the Middle East, from the
plan of 16 tales (`docs/stillroom_frame.md`, *The full shelf*). It
replaces the sealed teaser `sealed_alamut_1256`; the teaser on shelf III
is now `sealed_zimbabwe_1871`. Id: `alamut_1256`; jar label **Alamut,
1256**, its map pin on the rock of Alamut (Qazvin, Iran).

## Premise

In **1090** Hasan-i Sabbah took the castle of **Alamut**, on a rock above
a valley of the Alborz, without a battle: he had won over people inside,
and its lord was paid 3,000 gold dinars. For 166 years it was the seat of
the **Nizari Ismaili** state. Hasan lived there thirty-four years and
never came down; he kept the law strictly, improved the valley's fields
and water so it could feed itself, and founded a **library** of
manuscripts and scientific instruments. Scholars came to it; the
astronomer **Nasir al-Din al-Tusi** lived among the Nizaris for about
twenty years.

In **November 1256** the imam **Rukn al-Din Khurshah** surrendered to the
Mongol prince **Hulagu** at Maymun-Diz and was made to order his castles
to surrender. Alamut's commander hesitated, then gave the castle up in
**December 1256**. Hulagu's secretary, the historian **Ata-Malik
Juvayni**, went up to see the library. By his own account he took out
copies of the Qur'an, "other choice books" and the astronomical
instruments (astrolabes whole and in parts, armillary spheres), and
**burned the rest**. He read Hasan's own life story, used it in his
history, and burned it too. It has not survived. The Mongols found the
rock-cut storerooms full: tanks of **wine, vinegar and honey**, deep
enough that a man nearly drowned in the honey.

Forty years later **Marco Polo**, who had never been there, told Europe
what he "heard from sundry persons": that the "Old Man of the Mountain"
kept a secret **garden of paradise** where streams of **wine, milk, honey
and water** ran, and drugged young men so they would wake in it and later
kill for him; and that Hulagu besieged the castle from **1262** for **three
years** until it was starved out. The word *assassin* was later tied to
*hashish* (de Sacy, 1809), from an insult other Muslims had used for the
Nizaris ("rabble"), not a record of any drug.

The keeper's jar holds what the fortress really kept: not a garden but a
library, and the year it was burned.

## Tone and guardrails

- **The killings are not denied.** Nizari fida'is did kill political
  enemies (the vizier Nizam al-Mulk in 1092 is the most famous). The
  tale sets right the garden, the drug and the fall, not the killings;
  one paper says so plainly.
- **A living faith.** The Nizari Ismailis are a living community. No imam
  is shown or voiced; nothing of the faith is mocked. Polo's words
  ("heretics", "paradise") stay in his book, framed as a later hearsay
  account.
- **No violence shown.** The Mongols appear only as campfires far down
  the valley and as an order on paper. Khurshah's death on the road in
  1257 is told in one line, not shown.
- **Juvayni and Tusi are not judged.** Juvayni's own words say what he
  saved and burned; Tusi's choice to counsel surrender and go to Hulagu
  is stated, not weighed.
- **No invented content for the lost books.** The shelves show spines,
  empty niches, and what Juvayni names; the text of the lost books is
  never made up.
- **Legends are red herrings:** the garden of paradise, the drug, the
  three-year siege, 1262.

## Where it is set

- **A. Alamut, mid-December 1256** (recommended): the castle just given
  up, before the fire. Snow on the rock, the garrison gone, Mongol
  campfires far down the valley. The library's shelves half pulled out.
  Juvayni's pages (written about 1260) and Polo's book (1298) arrive as
  papers "that should not be here yet". The library can still be seen,
  and the tanks are still full, so both mechanics are what really
  happened.
- **B. The ruin today** (the Iranian excavations from 2004, UNESCO
  listing): bare rock, cut cisterns, pipe fragments. Truthful, but the
  library and the full tanks are gone; the tale would be told only
  through papers.

## New mechanics (this tale only)

- **The quire (`quire`).** The height of the tale. Juvayni's account
  lies as loose sheets: each sheet a folded pair of leaves (a bifolium).
  A book is a stack of such sheets nested one inside another, so one
  sheet holds a leaf near the front and its partner near the back. At the
  foot of each page the scribe wrote the **catchword**: the first word of
  the next page. Nest the sheets in the right order, and turn over any
  that lie the wrong way, until every catchword meets its next page; then
  the account can be read through. Four sheets (eight leaves). Unlike the
  timetable (`sequence`) or the fresco (`overlay`): each sheet fixes two
  places at once, one at each end of the book.
- **The tanks (`dip`).** Four tanks in the rock, too dark to see into.
  Lower the reed; you feel where it meets the surface (how full the tank
  is) and, drawing it out, see how it drips: honey in a slow thread,
  vinegar in quick thin drops, wine dark, water clear. Name each tank.
  Polo's list was wine, milk, honey and water; the tanks hold wine,
  vinegar, honey and water, and they are full: the castle was not starved
  out. Tactile, and unlike the probe (`scan`): nothing is mapped, each
  tank is named by how it behaves.

(Considered and set aside: an astrolabe that is used to read the hour,
too close to the stained-glass rings of `rotaryAlign`; Tusi's couple of
wheels, too close to the `crank`.)

## The seal

A new form, **`colophon`**: the closing lines at the end of a
manuscript, where the scribe writes what the book is and when it was
finished, the text narrowing to a point. Written in brown ink on
burnished paper, with a scorched edge.

Two sentences to choose from:

- **(a)** "The fortress kept a {library}, not a garden; {Juvayni} gave
  its books to the fire in {1256}." Three plain facts (recommended).
- **(b)** "Behind these walls lay no {garden} but a {library}, and
  {Juvayni} chose its books for the fire." The player writes the
  legend's own word in order to deny it, as the Flannan seal does.

Decoys: a garden of paradise, hashish, opium, Marco Polo, Hulagu, Tusi,
Hasan-i Sabbah, 1090, 1262, three years.

**Ending:** "Alamut was given up in December 1256. Its library was
burned; Hasan's own story survives only in the pages of the man who
burned it."

## Echoes and living things

- **Echoes:** a faceless librarian with a lamp between the niches; a
  garrison man at the gate, walking down. No imam, no Mongol, no Juvayni.
- **Living things (Alborz, December):** snow on the rock, dry thistles
  in the court, an eagle turning over the gorge (the "eagle's nest"), a
  mouse in the storerooms.

## As built

- **Map:** the court (start) opens on the tower (always) and the
  storerooms (with the commander's keys); the library is reached from
  the storerooms, up the tunnel, once the stores are counted. Its door on
  the court stays barred from within.

  ```
            tower
              │
  court ─ storerooms ─ library (up the tunnel)
  ```
- **Flow:** the order at the gate (*1256*). In the tower, Hasan's mat
  (*Hasan-i Sabbah*, *1090*), Polo's book (*Marco Polo*, *a garden of
  paradise*, *1262*, *three years*) and the commander's keys. The
  storerooms: the tanks (`dip`), behind a tally board that bars the
  tunnel until they are counted. The library: Tusi's corner (*al-Tusi*,
  *Hulagu*), the loose sheets (`quire`, gives *a library* and *Juvayni*);
  then a later paper on the book-stand (*hashish*; the fida'is and Nizam
  al-Mulk; Gerdkuh 1270; 1275). The keeper's slip lies under the
  astrolabe's empty throne once the later paper is read; the colophon is
  written at the gate.
- **The quire:** eight leaves, four sheets, starting with one catchword
  in place at most; from the outside in the sheets hold pages 1 and 8, 2
  and 7, 3 and 6, 4 and 5. The leaves paraphrase Juvayni in the first
  person; every leaf begins with its own word in all seven languages.
- **The tanks:** wine (0.86), vinegar (0.78), honey (0.9), water (0.7);
  the names offered are Polo's four and vinegar. The reed drips for six
  seconds after each dip.
- **Words:** *a library*, *Juvayni*, *1256* (the answers); *a garden of
  paradise*, *hashish*, *Hulagu*, *al-Tusi*, *Hasan-i Sabbah*, *Marco
  Polo*, *1090*, *1262*, *three years* (decoys).
- **Echoes:** a guard of the garrison by the parapet; a librarian with a
  lamp in the library. **Living things:** thistles in the snow, an eagle
  over the gorge and past the tower window, a mouse in the storerooms.
- **Audio:** music `alamut_snow`; effects `wind_rock`, `tank_cover`,
  `keys_jingle`, `eagle_cry`; interface `catchword`, `reed_touch`,
  `drip`, `drip_slow`.

## Things to watch when tested

- Whether the catchwords read at phone size, and whether the arcs under
  the pages make the nesting clear (a sheet's two leaves move together).
- Whether lowering the reed until it "meets" the surface is felt (the
  sound and the slowing), and whether the drips tell the liquids apart
  without the hint.
- Whether the storerooms' locked door sends the player to the tower
  soon enough, and whether the tally board across the tunnel is clear.
- Whether the eagle is seen, and not taken for something to tap.
