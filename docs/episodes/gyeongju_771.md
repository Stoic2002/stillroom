# Episode design: Gyeongju, 771 (the Emille Bell)

Status: **design draft** (2026-09-30), for the developer's approval before
anything is built. Shelf II, Korea, from the plan of 16 tales
(`docs/stillroom_frame.md`, *The full shelf*). Proposed id:
`gyeongju_771`; jar label **Gyeongju, 771**.

## Premise

Bongdeoksa temple, Gyeongju, the capital of Silla, on the winter night of
the **fourteenth day of the twelfth month of 771**. After years of work
under two kings, the great bronze bell for the late King Seongdeok is cast.
It has not yet been rung.

A later legend says the bell would not ring until a child was cast into
the bronze, and that it still cries *"emille"* for its mother. The record
tells another story. The bell's own inscription names the men who cast
it, and says nothing of any child. A tale of a child in a bell was told of
a bell in **Seoul**, not Gyeongju, in 1906. The name *Emille* first
appears in print in **1925**, in a children's story. A metal analysis in
**1998** found no phosphorus, the main element of bone. And the "cry" has
a name in the science of bells: a **beat**, two tones a hair apart in
pitch, made by the bronze being very slightly uneven.

The keeper gives the bell back to its makers: the founders by name, the
year it first rang, and what its cry really is.

Why it fits the Stillroom: a legend that gave a masterpiece a dead child
and took away the names of the men who made it. The truth is in the metal
and in the words cast on it.

## Tone and guardrails

- **No child, ever.** The legend is told only in papers that "should not
  be here yet" (1906, 1925), in a sentence each. No child or mother is
  drawn, echoed or voiced. The bell's sound is never made to sound like a
  voice; it swells and fades, as the beat does.
- **Honour the makers:** the founders and the writer of the inscription
  are named as the inscription names them, with their ranks. Nothing is
  invented about their lives.
- **The legend's origin is disputed; the tale does not take a side** on who
  first told it. It says only what is known: no record before the 1900s,
  first told of a Seoul bell (1906), first named *Emille* in print in 1925.
- **Legends are red herrings:** the child, "Emille", Seoul, the 1925 story.
- **Invented props are stated as invented:** the channel plan and the
  founders' tally board are the jar's own. The 1998 report is represented
  by a one-line summary of its finding, not a facsimile.

## New mechanics (Gyeongju only)

- **The pour (`pour`).** The mould's pouring cups, and above them clay
  channels from three furnaces. Each channel piece can be turned. Route
  the bronze so every cup fills and none spills into the sand; the
  furnaces are then opened together. A tile puzzle, after *The Room*'s
  mechanisms; tied to the fact that the bell took years to cast.
- **The hollow (`resonance`).** The bell in cross-section over its hollow.
  Pull the log striker back on its ropes and let it swing into the lotus
  seat; the ring is drawn as a trace running across the screen, loud,
  then fading. Deepen or fill the hollow (five depths) and strike again:
  at the right depth the trace runs past the mark. Tactile (drag and
  release) and heard as well as seen.
- **The cry (`beat`),** the height of the tale. The bell's rim seen from
  below, with eight places to strike. Each strike rings the bell; the
  trace swells and fades in waves, deeper or shallower depending on where
  it is struck. Find the one place where the swell is deepest: that is
  where the bronze is uneven, and the "cry" is its beat. After *Her
  Trees* (finding the one angle), and a listening puzzle unlike the swell
  (no timing: compare and choose).

## The seal

A new form, **`rubbing`**: an ink rubbing (*takbon*) taken from the bronze,
black paper with the letters left pale.

"Cast in {771} by {Park Jong-il} and three founders, the bell holds no
child: its cry is the {beat} of its bronze."

Decoys: a child, Emille, Seoul, 1925, Kim Pil-o, King Seongdeok, the
hollow.

**Ending:** the bell rings once more. "It rang over Gyeongju for more than
twelve hundred years, from a temple, a city gate, a museum. Its founders
cast their names into it. The cry was never a child's."

## Echoes and living things

- **Echoes:** founders with ladles at the casting pit; a monk at the
  pavilion. Faceless, as always. No child, no mother.
- **Living things (Gyeongju, midwinter night):** frosted grass in the
  yard; a rat in the shed. (A magpie on the pavilion roof would need a new
  creature; optional.)
